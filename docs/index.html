SECS, repackaged

Propose molecular structures from a molecular formula and one processed one-dimensional proton (1H) NMR spectrum.

This service repackages SECS, the work of Adrian Mirza, Luc Patiny, Kevin Maik Jablonka, and upstream contributors. We did not develop the scientific methods or train the supplied model. Our work connects them to the NMR API, handles uploaded spectra, and supports reproducible deployment, with adaptations maintained in pinned forks.

We also used the upstream GUI, elucidation.cheminfo.org (source), as a reference for spectrum conversion and published challenge fixtures. Our conversion tests use outputs from a pinned revision of its frontend. The GUI is a separate application, not the interface to this API provider; its supported inputs and results may differ.

Submit a job

You need access to an NMR API deployment that offers SECS. Use the client supplied by its operator; ask the operator for the client address and access if you do not have them. This page is documentation, not an upload form.

Choose the SECS provider and the analysis kind mol_from_1h_spectrum_formula. Attach a supported spectrum and put its molecular formula in the job instructions. If SECS is not listed, ask the deployment operator whether it is available.

For every job, a language-model interpreter receives the job instructions and attachment descriptions to choose one discovered spectrum representation, its processing, and the formula evidence. A formula taken from the job instructions must be quoted exactly; a formula taken from an attached structure must cite that discovered representation. The interpreter can inspect recognized data and relationships across every attachment linked to the job. These are sent to the deployment's configured language-model service; check with the operator before submitting confidential information.

Supported uploads

Preparing a submission

Multiple uploads
Multiple uploads are accepted. In the job input, name the intended upload or experiment path and formula source.
Vendor experiment folders
Keep Bruker and Varian files in their experiment folders.
Dense spectrum required
Provide dense 1D proton data. Peak tables may be reported but cannot replace a spectrum.

Spectrum data

Processed JCAMP-DX
Supported input: JCAMP-DX XYDATA or NTUPLES. Supported cases include LINK blocks and AFFN, FIX, SQZ, DIF, DIFDUP, and PAC numeric encodings.
Processed Bruker
Supported input: Bruker 1r with its procs file. Keep 1r and procs together in the same pdata directory. The processed pair does not require acqus; include title when it is available.
JCAMP-DX FID
Supported input: complex JCAMP-DX FIDs with acquisition parameters.
Bruker FID
Supported input: Bruker fid with acqus and zero group delay. Keep fid and acqus together in one experiment directory. Other group-delay profiles are reported as unsupported.
Varian FID
Supported input: Varian fid with procpar where reffrq=sfrq and rfl-rfp=sw/2. Keep fid and procpar together in one experiment directory. Other reference profiles are reported as unsupported.
Processed JEOL JDF
Supported input: processed JEOL JDF with a ppm axis (synthetic qualification only).
NMRium v21
Supported input: NMRium v21 states with dense proton data or attached JCAMP-DX resources. Keep embedded resources with the state; stored spectrum shifts are applied once.

Formula evidence

Formula in the job input
Supported input: a formula written verbatim in the job input.
Formula from a structure
Supported input: a formula calculated from a selected MOL, SDF or SMILES attachment.

Supplied annotations

NMReDATA relationships
Supported input: NMReDATA links between a structure, its JCAMP-DX resource and proton assignments. The linked spectrum resource must be attached and readable. Assignments are preserved as supplied context; they do not change the spectrum sent to SECS.

Example: a Bruker experiment

For the strychnine example files used by our Bruker regression test, ZIP F3697/1/pdata/1/, including 1r, procs, and title. Attach that ZIP and write:

Analyse the sole processed 1H spectrum in the attached ZIP.
Molecular formula: C21H22N2O2.

What the result means

SECS retrieves candidates from a molecular index and refines them with a genetic algorithm. The result includes ranked candidate SMILES (text representations of molecular structures), scores, the selected input and formula, selection explanations, and search settings.

Higher scores rank better within a search; they are not probabilities that a structure is correct. These are candidates for chemical review, not validated structure assignments. The example above demonstrates input preparation, not a guarantee of recovering strychnine.

If retrieval finds no starting molecules, no elucidation is produced. The attempt fails with no_starting_candidates: Graph GA refinement did not run and evaluated no structures. The deployment operator retains the full report under the attempt identifier; ask the operator for it using that identifier. This search limitation does not establish that the formula is invalid or that no matching structure exists.

The report's analysis.retrieval counts show where retrieval stopped: neighbours_returned counts the molecules returned by the index search; formula_matches counts those that passed its formula filter; starting_candidates counts those selected for refinement after the population limit. These counts do not show whether suitable molecules exist elsewhere in the index. The configured retrieval limit is not the number of molecules actually returned.

A successful attempt produced ranked candidate proposals. It does not establish a validated structure assignment. Earlier attempts may show no_starting_candidates as successful; those records are not rewritten.

If the interpreter cannot select usable input, the attempt fails with cannot_analyse: no analysis was produced. Its failure message includes the interpreter's explanation. That explanation is the model's account, not proof that a reader ran or that your spectrum is invalid. The deployment operator retains the full report and recorded rejection reasons under the attempt identifier. Older attempts may show this outcome as a successful result; that did not mean structures were found.

A failed attempt reports an error or interruption, for example an outage or an interpretation limit. Failure status alone does not establish whether computation finished. Read its failure message and share the job and attempt identifiers with the deployment operator if you need help. Do not assume that it means the spectrum is invalid, or that selection history will be available.
